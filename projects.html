Research Projects

Pan-Amazonian Project – Knowledge Exchange for Tropical Forest Monitoring and Sustainable Management

MCTI/CNPq Public Call No. 03/2025 – PRÓ-AMAZÔNIA
Brazil–Ecuador collaboration
Period: December 2025 – Present

This project, AmazonForestNet – Amazon Forest Management & Monitoring Network , aims to strengthen scientific collaboration between Brazil and Ecuador in the monitoring and sustainable management of Amazonian tropical forests. The initiative focuses on integrating permanent forest plots, long-term monitoring networks, and knowledge exchange among research institutions, government agencies, and forest management stakeholders.

My contributions include:

• Planning and coordination of binational field campaigns between Brazil and Ecuador, including priority area selection, operational planning, logistics, and institutional coordination among EMBRAPA, FAO Ecuador, IKIAM, and local partners.

• Standardization and harmonization of methodological protocols for the establishment, remeasurement, and monitoring of permanent plots in managed Amazonian forests, following the guidelines of the Amazon Managed Forest Research Network (REDFLOR).

• Modelling forest growth, mortality, and recruitment of commercial tree species using permanent plot data and statistical approaches to support sustainable forest management decisions.

• Training and capacity building in the use of the MFT – Tropical Forest Monitoring software for forest inventory data management, validation, and analysis.

• Scientific coordination and knowledge transfer among universities, research centres, governmental institutions, and forest management organizations in Brazil and Ecuador.

Project - Monitoring of Giant Trees in Amazonia (MIAG)

Program: Long-Term Ecological Research Program (PELD)
Institutions: Universidade do Estado do Amapá (UEAP) and Instituto Federal do Amapá (IFAP)
Period: May 2025 – Present

The MIAG project investigates the ecological role of giant trees in Amazonian forests, focusing on forest structure, biomass accumulation, carbon storage, biodiversity patterns, and ecosystem responses to environmental change. The project integrates field inventories, long-term ecological monitoring, and remote sensing approaches to understand the distribution and ecological importance of exceptional trees across tropical forests.

Associated platform:

The project contributes to the GigaFlor – Amazon Giant Forests Observatory , a research initiative dedicated to mapping giant trees, forest carbon stocks, and climate resilience across Amazonian tropical forests.

The GigaFlor Observatory integrates forest inventory data, environmental monitoring, functional ecology, and spatial approaches to advance knowledge about giant tree ecosystems and their role in Amazon conservation.

My contributions include:

• Planning and coordination of ecological research activities within the PELD framework, focusing on giant tree structure and forest dynamics.

• Field campaigns in protected areas including Parque Nacional Montanhas do Tumucumaque and Estação Ecológica do Jari, collecting data on forest regeneration, biomass structure, and soil–vegetation relationships.

• Analysis of ecological and forest datasets using R, Python, and Google Earth Engine, integrating field inventories and remote sensing approaches.

• Development of ecological models investigating large tree distributions, carbon stocks, and vegetation responses under climate change scenarios.

• Collaboration with national and international research institutions, including universities, research centres, governmental organizations, and conservation agencies.

Tropical Managed Forests Observatory (TmFO)

International research network: Tropical Managed Forests Observatory (TmFO)

Research activities on sustainable forest management in the Brazilian Amazon, including permanent forest plots and long-term monitoring initiatives led by Embrapa Amazônia Oriental, are integrated into the broader framework of the Tropical Managed Forests Observatory (TmFO).

TmFO is a pan-tropical research network dedicated to understanding the long-term ecological consequences of forest management and logging across tropical forests. The network integrates more than 600 permanent forest plots distributed across Latin America, Africa, and Southeast Asia, providing a unique framework for evaluating forest recovery, biomass dynamics, timber volume recovery, and changes in species composition over time.

Within this international network, research in Amazonian managed forests contributes to improving evidence-based sustainable forest management practices, supporting strategies that maintain forest ecosystem functions, carbon storage, biodiversity conservation, and long-term economic viability.

My contributions include:

• Integration of permanent forest inventory data from Amazonian managed forests into long-term ecological monitoring approaches.

• Analysis of forest growth, mortality, recruitment, biomass dynamics, and carbon recovery following reduced-impact logging.

• Collaboration with researchers from Embrapa Amazônia Oriental and national and international partners involved in tropical forest monitoring networks.

• Application of quantitative approaches using forest inventories, remote sensing, and statistical modelling to support sustainable forest management strategies.
