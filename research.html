Research

My research focuses on understanding the structure, dynamics, and functioning of tropical forests, with emphasis on Amazonian ecosystems. I investigate how environmental gradients, forest management practices, and climate change influence biodiversity, biomass accumulation, carbon storage, and the resilience of tropical forests.

My work combines long-term ecological monitoring, forest inventories, remote sensing technologies, and quantitative modelling approaches to improve our understanding of tropical forest conservation and sustainable management.

Main Research Areas

🌳 Giant Trees and Forest Structure

I study the ecological role of giant trees in Amazonian forests, investigating their contribution to forest structure, biodiversity, aboveground biomass, carbon stocks, and ecosystem functioning. This research integrates field measurements, forest inventories, and remote sensing approaches to identify the distribution and ecological importance of exceptional trees.

🌎 Sustainable Forest Management

My research evaluates the long-term consequences of tropical forest management, including reduced-impact logging, forest recovery, growth dynamics, mortality, and recruitment processes. The objective is to develop scientific knowledge supporting sustainable management practices that maintain ecosystem functions, carbon storage, and biodiversity.

🌿 Forest Biomass and Carbon Dynamics

I investigate patterns of aboveground biomass variation across tropical forests and environmental gradients, evaluating the relationships between tree size, species composition, forest structure, and carbon accumulation. These studies contribute to improving biomass estimation and understanding the role of tropical forests in global carbon cycles.

🛰 Remote Sensing and Forest Monitoring

I apply remote sensing technologies, including airborne LiDAR, satellite products, and Google Earth Engine workflows, to characterize forest structure, canopy height, biomass distribution, and landscape-scale ecological patterns. These approaches allow the integration of field observations with spatial monitoring of tropical forests.

📈 Long-Term Ecological Monitoring

Through permanent forest plots and ecological monitoring networks, I study forest dynamics over time, including tree growth, mortality, regeneration, species turnover, and ecosystem responses to environmental changes.

Research Questions

  • How do environmental gradients influence forest structure, biomass, and carbon storage in tropical forests?
  • What ecological factors determine the occurrence and persistence of giant trees in Amazonian ecosystems?
  • How do managed tropical forests recover after logging and maintain ecosystem functions over decades?
  • How can remote sensing technologies improve forest monitoring and carbon assessment?
  • How can long-term ecological networks support evidence-based conservation and sustainable forest management?

Research Approaches

My research integrates multiple methodological approaches:

  • Permanent forest inventories and long-term ecological plots
  • Tree allometry, biomass estimation, and carbon assessment
  • Airborne LiDAR and satellite remote sensing
  • Spatial analysis and environmental modelling
  • Statistical modelling using R and Python
  • Google Earth Engine applications for forest monitoring

Study Regions

Current research activities are concentrated in Amazonian forests, including protected areas and managed forests in Brazil, with collaborations extending to Ecuador and other tropical forest regions.

  • Amazonian forests of Amapá, Brazil
  • Parque Nacional Montanhas do Tumucumaque
  • Estação Ecológica do Jari
  • Managed tropical forests monitored through long-term networks
  • Pan-Amazonian research collaborations
